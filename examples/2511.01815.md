# KV Cache Transform Coding for Compact Storage in LLM Inference

**Authors:** Konrad Staniszewski, Adrian Łańcucki  
**arXiv ID:** 2511.01815  
**Published:** 2025-11-03 (last updated 2025-11-03)  
**Categories:** not available  
**Links:** [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01815) · [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.01815)

## Why this paper matters
KVTC introduces a lightweight transform‑coding pipeline that compresses the key‑value caches used during LLM inference, cutting memory usage by up to 20× (and over 40× for some scenarios) without changing model weights. By shrinking these caches, services can keep more conversations on‑GPU, reduce off‑loading costs, and avoid costly recomputation of stale caches, directly improving throughput and latency in production deployments. The method works across a range of model sizes (1.5B‑70B) and maintains accuracy on benchmarks such as GSM8K, MMLU, and QASPER. Practitioners gain a drop‑in compression tool that integrates with existing inference stacks and complements other cache‑management techniques.

## Problem
Large KV caches in LLM inference consume gigabytes of GPU memory, creating a latency‑throughput trade‑off between keeping caches for fast reuse and off‑loading or recomputing them. Existing cache‑compression methods either offer modest size reductions, require per‑prompt computation, or degrade accuracy, limiting their practicality for production serving.

## Method
- Compute a single PCA basis (V) from a calibration dataset and reuse it for all KV caches
- Project keys and values onto the PCA basis to decorrelate features
- Allocate variable‑bit quantization per principal component via dynamic programming
- Entropy‑code the quantized symbols with DEFLATE
- Store compressed caches on‑GPU or off‑GPU; decompress on‑the‑fly during decoding

## Key results
- kvtc achieves up to 20× compression while maintaining reasoning and long‑context accuracy
- Compression ratios of 40× or higher are reported for specific use cases
- On Llama 3.1 8B, kvtc16× reaches 57.8% GSM8K accuracy versus 56.8% vanilla (within 1 point)
- kvtc reduces total latency from 3098 ms (vanilla recompute) to 380 ms TTFT on Mistral NeMo 12B
- Calibration for a 12B model completes in ~10 minutes on an H100 GPU

## Limitations
- Frobenius‑norm reconstruction error is used as a proxy for downstream task performance, which may not reliably predict accuracy across all tasks _(stated by the authors)_
- Evaluation is performed on simulated multi‑turn benchmarks; real‑world interaction patterns and content distributions may differ _(stated by the authors)_
- Calibration requires processing ~200K tokens on a single GPU, which could be costly for very large models or frequent re‑calibration _(inferred, not stated by the authors)_
- Compression and decompression overheads are still noticeable (e.g., 379 ms total compression time), and further speed‑ups rely on kernel fusion not yet implemented _(stated by the authors)_

## Questions worth asking
- How does KVTC perform on models larger than 70 B or on encoder‑decoder architectures?
- Can the PCA basis be updated incrementally to adapt to shifting data distributions without full re‑calibration?
- What is the impact of KVTC when combined with other cache‑reduction techniques such as token eviction or sparsification?
- How does the method scale in a multi‑node, pipeline‑parallel deployment where caches are sharded across GPUs?
- Would alternative proxies (e.g., task‑specific validation loss) provide better guidance for selecting compression ratios?

## Q&A transcript

**Q:** What compression ratio does KVTC achieve?

**A:** KVTC can reach a wide range of compression ratios depending on the model, task and configuration. In the main experiments it attains very high ratios of **32× and 64×** while still preserving accuracy, and it even maintains comparable performance at about **20×** compression [2][4]. The underlying DEFLATE loss‑less step adds an additional average factor of **≈1.23×** on top of the lossy quantization stage [2]. Thus, KVTC’s overall compression ratio varies from roughly **20× up to 64×**, with an extra ≈1.23× boost from the final lossless step.
